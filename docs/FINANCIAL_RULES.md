# Financial rules and definitions

These are the rules the database uses (see `supabase/migrations`). The figures in the app, reports, CSV exports and AI Advisor all come from the same SQL functions, so they agree with each other.

> These are management-accounting figures for running a shop. They are not statutory financial statements, and the figures have not been reviewed for compliance with OHADA/SYSCOHADA or Cameroonian tax rules. See [LIMITATIONS.md](LIMITATIONS.md).

## Precision and rounding

| Kind | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Money amounts (prices, totals, payments) | `numeric(14,2)` | FCFA has no subunit in practice; the UI shows whole francs, but the database keeps 2 decimals so allocations add up exactly |
| Unit costs, average cost, stock value | `numeric(18,4)` | Avoids rounding drift in weighted averages |
| Quantities | `numeric(14,3)` | Fractional quantities only when the product allows them (e.g. kg, litres) |
| Plan prices | `numeric(12,0)` | |

No floating-point arithmetic is used for money. Totals are computed on the server; the client's figures are only previews.

## Units

Each product has a **base unit** (e.g. piece). Alternative units (carton, pack, crate…) have a **factor**: the number of base units in one unit. Stock is always stored in base units. The server looks up the factor itself and never trusts one sent by the client. Once a unit has been used, it can't be deleted, and its factor can't change.

## Inventory costing: weighted average cost (perpetual)

- Each product stores `qty_on_hand` and `stock_value`; **average cost = stock_value ÷ qty_on_hand**.
- **Inflows** (opening stock, purchase receipts, restocked customer returns, found stock, positive count differences) add `quantity × acquisition cost` and recompute the average.
- **Outflows** (sales, supplier returns, damage, expiry, missing stock, negative count differences) remove stock at the **current average cost**.
- The cost used is written on the stock movement and on the sale line. **Past profit is never recalculated with later costs.**
- Only `app.apply_movement()` can change stock figures. It also writes the immutable ledger row (`stock_movements`). A trigger blocks direct API writes to those columns.
- Batches/expiry dates are tracked first-expiry-first-out for **quantity only**. Costing stays weighted average.
- When a product has no known cost (sold before any cost was recorded), its cost counts as 0. The dashboard and reports then flag profit as **estimated** (`data_quality`).

## Wholesale purchases and landed cost

- Statuses: `draft → ordered → partially_received → received`, or `cancelled` (only while nothing has been received).
- **Landed unit cost** of a line = `line_total × (1 + f) ÷ (quantity × unit factor)`, where `f = (transport + other costs − purchase discount) ÷ purchase subtotal`. Extra costs and discounts are spread over the lines in proportion to their value.
- Receiving more than was ordered is rejected. Closing a purchase short creates a supplier credit for the part not received.
- **Return to supplier:** stock leaves at the current average cost (as any outflow). The supplier credit is valued at the line's landed cost. Any difference between the two shows up as a stock gain or loss on the movement.

## Supplier balance (what you owe)

```
Σ totals of purchases in ordered / partially_received / received
− Σ supplier credits (returns, short deliveries, adjustments)
− Σ payments to the supplier
+ Σ refunds received from the supplier
```

Draft and cancelled purchases create no debt. Payments are allocated to purchases in a fixed order: the purchase the payment was recorded against comes first, then the oldest outstanding purchase. The order is recomputed from the ledger, so it's always the same.

## Sales

- **Idempotent:** every sale carries a `client_ref` generated by the device. Re-sending the same sale (double tap, offline retry) returns the original sale instead of creating a second one. Tested with 4 concurrent submissions → 1 sale.
- **Atomic:** stock check, stock movement, sale, payments and customer balance are written in one transaction. If stock is insufficient, nothing is saved.
- **Prices and tax:** prices include any configured sales tax. Tax is extracted from the price (`tax = amount × rate ÷ (100 + rate)`).
- **Discounts:** line discounts first, then a sale-level discount spread over the lines in proportion to their value. Cashiers are limited to the business's maximum cashier discount %.
- **Channel:** every sale records where it was made: in person, WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, phone, other, or `marketplace`. The marketplace channel is set only for sales created by delivering a marketplace order, so an online order is never recorded twice. Reports → Channels shows revenue (net of returns), cost, gross profit, paid and outstanding per channel.
- **Sale date:** a sale can be dated earlier (e.g. recorded after a WhatsApp delivery), never in the future.
- **Sale costs:** a delivery the seller paid for is entered with the sale and recorded once as a paid `delivery` expense linked to it. It is an operating expense, not cost of goods.
- **Payments are seller-confirmed:** every sale payment is recorded by the business (cash, MTN, Orange, bank, other). Only BizPilot subscription payments are verified with a provider (Fapshi).
- **Credit / partial payment:** needs a named customer, the `customers_credit` feature, the `customers.credit` permission, and must stay within the customer's credit limit. Walk-in customers must pay in full.

## Returns and refunds

- A customer return references the original sale lines and can't exceed what was sold minus earlier returns.
- **Restocked** returns put the goods back at the **cost captured on the original sale line**, which reverses the original COGS. Returns that aren't restocked (damaged) do not reduce COGS.
- A walk-in sale return must be refunded in full. For a named customer, any amount not refunded reduces what they owe.
- **Cancelling a sale** is a full return of what is left (to stock if restockable), with a refund of what was paid. The sale keeps its number, lines and history, and gets `cancelled_at` and a reason. Marketplace sales are refunded from their order instead.
- Nothing is deleted or edited in place. Corrections are new counter-entries (returns, reversals, voided expenses with a counter-entry), and each one is audited.

## Customer balance (what a customer owes you)

```
Σ (sale total − returned amount + refunded amount) − Σ payments received
```

Payments are allocated in a fixed order: the sale the payment was recorded against first, then the oldest unpaid sale.

## Period figures (dashboard, Profit & Loss)

All periods use the business's local time zone (Africa/Douala). Date ranges include both end dates. Sales count on the sale date; returns count on the return date.

| Figure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Gross sales | Σ sale totals (tax included) |
| Discounts | Line discounts + sale discounts + price reductions from list price |
| Returns | Σ customer return totals in the period |
| Tax collected | Tax on sales − tax on returns |
| **Net sales** | (Σ sale totals − tax) − (Σ return totals − tax on returns) |
| **Cost of goods sold** | Σ cost captured on sale lines − cost of restocked returns |
| **Gross profit** | Net sales − COGS |
| Gross margin % | Gross profit ÷ net sales × 100 (blank when net sales ≤ 0) |
| Operating expenses | Σ expenses dated in the period (not voided), whether or not they are paid yet |
| Stock losses | Net value of damaged, expired, missing, found, correction and count movements |
| **Estimated operating profit** | Gross profit − operating expenses − stock losses. Labelled *estimated* because it only includes what was recorded |
| Purchases | Σ totals of purchases dated in the period in ordered / partially received / received status |
| Inventory value | Σ `stock_value` (quantity × average cost) now; the trend is rebuilt from the movement ledger |
| Cash received / paid | From `payments` by date paid (sales, customer payments, refunds, supplier payments, expense payments) |

"vs previous period" compares with the period of the same length immediately before.

## Analytics rules

| Analysis | Rule |
|---|---|
| Best sellers / product performance | Units, net revenue, COGS and gross profit per product in the period, from sale lines minus returns |
| Category performance | Same, grouped by category (products without a category show as *Uncategorised*) |
| **Restocking** | Average daily sales over the look-back window (default 30 days). Reorder point = daily sales × (lead time + safety days). Target = daily sales × (lead time + safety days + planning days), at least the minimum stock. Recommended quantity = target − (on hand + on order), raised to the supplier's minimum order quantity. **Urgent:** out of stock, or fewer days of stock than the lead time. **Soon:** fewer days than lead time + safety days, or at/below minimum stock. **Confidence:** low with fewer than 5 sales or less than 14 days of history, medium below 20 sales, high otherwise. Defaults: lead time 3, safety 3, planning 14 days (Settings → Operations) |
| **Slow movers** | Active products with stock, older than the window (default 30 days), that had **no sales** in the window, or would take **more than 90 days** to sell at the window's rate |
| Inventory aging | Remaining stock is matched to the most recent inflows (newest stock is assumed still on hand). Value buckets: 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days |
| Weak margins | Products with a period gross margin below the business threshold (default 10%) |
| Price below cost | Active products with stock whose selling price is below the current average cost |
| Supplier comparison | Per supplier: number and value of purchases, deliveries on time vs tracked, average lead time, returns, balance owed; per product: average, lowest and last landed unit cost by supplier |
| Purchasing efficiency | For each product received in the period: quantity bought vs sold since, classified as *no sales after purchase*, *possible overbuying*, *stocked out* or *balanced* |
| Discrepancies / losses | Count differences and loss movements by product and reason |
| Weekday pattern | Average net sales and number of sales per weekday over the days in the period |

Each insight on the dashboard and in the AI Advisor shows the **period analysed**, the **evidence** (figures and products), and its **limitations** (e.g. "only 1 sale recorded", "costs estimated for some products"). With too little data, BizPilot says so ("Not enough sales to analyse yet") instead of drawing a conclusion.

## AI Advisor

- The server builds the context (`advisor_context`) from the same functions above and checks membership and permissions with the user's own JWT. The model only sees that summary, never raw tables.
- The model is told to use only the figures provided, cite the period, separate facts from suggestions, and say when data is insufficient.
- The Advisor **cannot take actions**. It never places orders, changes prices, issues refunds or starts payments.
- If the AI is unavailable (no key, error, refusal, monthly quota reached), the app answers from fixed rules over the same figures and labels the answer as rule-based.

## Plans and subscription states

The state is computed from dates every time it is read, so no scheduled job is needed: `trial → active → renewal_due (last N days) → grace (N days after end) → expired`, plus `cancelled` (runs to period end) and `suspended`. When paid features lapse, **no data is ever deleted**. The business falls back to Free features and limits, and history older than the plan's window is hidden until it upgrades again.
